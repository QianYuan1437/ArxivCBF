How to Train Your Neural Control Barrier Function: Learning Safety Filters for Complex Input-Constrained Systems

Oswin So, Zachary Serlin, Makai Mann, Jake Gonzales, Kwesi J. Rutledge et al.

Control barrier functions (CBFs) have become popular as a safety filter to guarantee the safety of nonlinear dynamical systems for arbitrary inputs. However, it is difficult to construct functions that satisfy the CBF constraints for high relative degree systems with input constraints. To address these challenges, recent work has explored learning CBFs using neural networks via neural CBFs (NCBFs). However, such methods face difficulties when scaling to higher dimensional systems under input constraints. In this work, we first identify challenges that NCBFs face during training. Next, to address these challenges, we propose policy neural CBFs (PNCBFs), a method of constructing CBFs by learning the value function of a nominal policy, and show that the value function of the maximum-over-time cost is a CBF. We demonstrate the effectiveness of our method in simulation on a variety of systems ranging from toy linear systems to a jet aircraft with a 16-dimensional state space. Finally, we validate our approach on a two-agent quadcopter system on hardware under tight input constraints.

A Survey on the Control Lyapunov Function and Control Barrier Function for Nonlinear-Affine Control Systems

Bo-Qian Li, Shi-Ping Wen, Zheng Yan, Guang-Hui Wen, Ting-Wen Huang

This survey provides a brief overview on the control Lyapunov function (CLF) and control barrier function (CBF) for general nonlinear-affine control systems. The problem of control is formulated as an optimization problem where the optimal control policy is derived by solving a constrained quadratic programming (QP) problem. The CLF and CBF respectively characterize the stability objective and the safety objective for the nonlinear control systems. These objectives imply important properties including controllability, convergence, and robustness of control problems. Under this framework, optimal control corresponds to the minimal solution to a constrained QP problem. When uncertainties are explicitly considered, the setting of the CLF and CBF is proposed to study the input-to-state stability and input-to-state safety and to analyze the effect of disturbances. The recent theoretic progress and novel applications of CLF and CBF are systematically reviewed and discussed in this paper. Finally, we provide research directions that are significant for the advance of knowledge in this area.

This paper presents an efficient and safe method to avoid static and dynamic obstacles based on LiDAR. First, point cloud is used to generate a real-time local grid map for obstacle detection. Together with control Lyapunov function (CLF), it forms a safety-critical control strategy, named CLF-CBF-QP, which can mediate between achieving the control objective and ensuring safety, while being executable in real-time. Zahra Marvi, Bahare Kiumarsi

This article presents a learning‐based barrier certified method to learn safe optimal controllers that guarantee operation of safety‐critical systems within their safe regions while providing an optimal performance. The cost function that encodes the designer's objectives is augmented with a control barrier function (CBF) to ensure safety and optimality. A damping coefficient is incorporated into the CBF which specifies the trade‐off between safety and optimality. The proposed formulation provides a look‐ahead and proactive safety planning and results in a smooth transition of states within the feasible set. That is, instead of applying an optimal controller and intervening with it only if the safety constraints are violated, the safety is planned and optimized along with the performance to minimize the intervention with the optimal controller. The efficacy of the proposed safe RL control design approach is demonstrated on the lane keeping as an automotive control problem.

Control Barrier Function Based Quadratic Programs for Safety Critical Systems

A. Ames, Xiangru Xu, J. Grizzle, P. Tabuada

Safety critical systems involve the tight coupling between potentially conflicting control objectives and safety constraints. As a means of creating a formal framework for controlling systems of this form, and with a view toward automotive applications, this paper develops a methodology that allows safety conditions—expressed as control barrier functions—to be unified with performance objectives—expressed as control Lyapunov functions—in the context of real-time optimization-based controllers. Safety conditions are specified in terms of forward invariance of a set, and are verified via two novel generalizations of barrier functions; in each case, the existence of a barrier function satisfying Lyapunov-like conditions implies forward invariance of the set, and the relationship between these two classes of barrier functions is characterized. In addition, each of these formulations yields a notion of control barrier function (CBF), providing inequality constraints in the control input that, when satisfied, again imply forward invariance of the set. Through these constructions, CBFs can naturally be unified with control Lyapunov functions (CLFs) in the context of a quadratic program (QP); this allows for the achievement of control objectives (represented by CLFs) subject to conditions on the admissible states of the system (represented by CBFs). The mediation of safety and performance through a QP is demonstrated on adaptive cruise control and lane keeping, two automotive control problems that present both safety and performance considerations coupled with actuator bounds.

Safe Output-Regulation Flows for Constrained Optimization via Control Barrier Functions

Zihao Ren, Bingqi Liu, Lei Wang, Hongye Su, Guodong Shi

Control-theoretic approaches to constrained optimization have been widely studied by interpreting optimization algorithms as dynamical systems. This paper formulates constrained optimization as an output-regulation problem with state constraints, where equality-constraint residuals are treated as regulated outputs and inequality constraints as state constraints. Proportional--integral feedback is employed to drive the equality-constraint residuals asymptotically to zero, while a control barrier function (CBF) safety filter is used to ensure inequality safety throughout the optimization process. We establish feasibility of the CBF filtering program throughout the inequality-feasible set and local exponential convergence of the resulting flow. Under stronger global conditions, we further prove convergence from an entire connected component of the inequality-feasible set. Since exact implementation of the flow requires solving a CBF quadratic program at each state, we further develop a flow that approximates the CBF solution through multiplier updates. Moreover, we provide a unified formulation encompassing the two proposed flows and projected primal--dual dynamics. Numerical examples demonstrate convergence, safety, and computational tradeoffs of the proposed flows.

Nested Field-of-View Barriers for Teleoperated Multi-Robot Relay Chains

Arthur Vangasse, Theo Cruz, Thales Soares, André Cid, Vinícius Gonçalves et al.

Teleoperation of mobile robots in communication-denied environments requires maintaining a communication link while allowing the operator to maneuver freely. We propose a shared-autonomy framework in which a teleoperated leader is followed by a chain of autonomous robots that relays a line of sight to a fixed base station, a geometric proxy for the link. Connectivity is enforced geometrically through visibility-based Control Barrier Functions (CBFs), with each agent computing its control input locally through a quadratic program. A strict visibility constraint guarantees connectivity, but a follower with no independent motion objective remains stationary, stalling the leader at the boundary of the safe set. Relaxing it preserves mobility at the cost of the guarantee. Our framework resolves this conflict through nested hard-soft visibility constraints: a full field-of-view (FoV) constraint enforced strictly to guarantee connectivity and a contracted FoV constraint imposed on each follower as a relaxed behavioral objective that drives it inward before the connectivity boundary is reached. We prove feasibility of the local quadratic programs and forward invariance of the connected, collision-free set under the resulting distributed controller. The contraction parameter shapes follower behavior without affecting the guarantee, which follows from the strict constraint alone. The framework is evaluated in confined and cluttered environments on two simulated systems: a kinematic integrator and software-in-the-loop Crazyflie quadrotors. Compared with a baseline tuned to reach the goal in comparable time, the framework keeps the links far closer to the connected set and moves the followers less.

Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) shows promise in coordinating connected and automated vehicles but offers no safety guarantee. Although Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) can add this guarantee, their optimization problem can become infeasible in practice due to control bounds. Moreover, they often overapproximate vehicle and road geometry, which makes them conservative. This paper proposes the Backup-Horizon CBF (BH-CBF), which evaluates safety along a horizon until all vehicles stop. BH-CBF is recursively feasible under control bounds, and it considers the exact vehicle and road geometry. In recorded roundabout traffic with 1264 human-driven vehicles over 115 km, our safeguarded policies shorten the median travel time by 14% compared with the human drivers, without any collision.

Algebraic Characterization of Time-Varying Set Invariance

S. B. Alaoui, E. J. Wafo Wembe, A. Saoud

The verification of temporal forward invariance for dynamical systems subject to time-varying constraints is a foundational problem in the analysis of safety-critical systems. Existing methodologies rely on restrictive geometric assumptions, such as the Linear Independence Constraint Qualification (LICQ), or suffer from exponential computational complexity when applied to time-varying polytopes. This paper establishes a rigorous, unified, and computationally tractable framework for temporal safety verification. First, utilizing tools from nonsmooth analysis, we prove that a Nagumo-type geometric inclusion evaluated strictly on the boundary of the augmented graph is both necessary and sufficient for temporal forward invariance. Second, we provide an algebraic characterization of this condition requiring only the weaker Abadie Constraint Qualification (ACQ), generalizing standard Control Barrier Function (CBF) boundary conditions to non-smooth, multi-constraint domains. Finally, for the practical class of Linear Time-Invariant (LTI) systems with time-varying polytopic constraints, we deploy Farkas' Lemma to eliminate exponential vertex enumeration. By introducing a Lipschitz-bounded time-discretization scheme, we reduce the infinite-dimensional continuous-time verification problem to a finite, highly tractable set of decoupled Linear Programs (LPs).

Autonomous inspection of a resident space object is usually posed as a contest between classical next-best-view planning and reinforcement learning. We argue that the choice of algorithm is the least informative of the design axes. From Local to Global: Sheaf-Theoretic Control Barrier Functions on Manifolds

Keith A. Currier, Wilmer Leal, Brandon C. Fallin, James Fairbanks, Warren E. Dixon

Control barrier functions (CBFs) provide formal safety guarantees for dynamical systems by rendering a safe set forward-invariant via pointwise-optimal feedback control. While the CBF framework is well established for dynamical systems in Euclidean space, there exist gaps for systems evolving on non-Euclidean manifolds. For systems modeled as constrained control differential inclusions, we introduce the Sheaf-CBF: a sheaf-theoretic framework for CBFs on smooth manifolds and vector bundles. The differential inclusions are formulated intrinsically on the control bundle, and analogous regularity properties in the Euclidean case are established fiberwise under local trivializations. Regularity conditions are given under which a pointwise-optimal feedback law is single-valued and continuous over local charts. Framed as a local-to-global optimization problem, sheaf-theoretic restriction and gluing compatibility conditions on overlapping charts are shown to uniquely determine a continuous global section of the control bundle, thereby guaranteeing forward invariance of the safe set. Numerical simulations on a nontrivial M{ö}bius strip demonstrate that failure of sheaf compatibility results in violations of safety, whereas the Sheaf-CBF maintains forward invariance.

Enforcing a Strict Safety Margin in LLM Text Generation via Barrier Lyapunov Control

Anand Singh, Shubham Singh, Anjali Tiwari, Katsuki Fujisawa

Large language models are increasingly deployed without formal guarantees against toxic text generation. Shubham Singh, Anjali Tiwari, Anand Singh, Katsuki Fujisawa

This paper presents a robust control framework for safe navigation of a unicycle robot to a goal among multiple static obstacles under an unknown, time-varying disturbance. The controller solves a single real-time quadratic program (QP) that combines a goal-reaching term with one safety term per obstacle. The goal-reaching term is a cross-track control Lyapunov function built from body-frame tracking errors rather than a bearing angle, which keeps its Lie derivatives bounded everywhere, including the goal. We further derive the obstacle separation below which the gap between two obstacles becomes too narrow to pass through under the robust constraints. For such gaps, a learned virtual goal for the Lyapunov function steers the robot around the obstacles without compromising safety. Simulations, a Monte Carlo study, and a comparison against a sampled-data robust baseline support the theoretical results.

Control Barrier Function Based Quadratic Programs for Safety Critical Systems

A. Ames, Xiangru Xu, J. Grizzle, P. Tabuada

Safety critical systems involve the tight coupling between potentially conflicting control objectives and safety constraints. As a means of creating a formal framework for controlling systems of this form, and with a view toward automotive applications, this paper develops a methodology that allows safety conditions—expressed as control barrier functions—to be unified with performance objectives—expressed as control Lyapunov functions—in the context of real-time optimization-based controllers. Safety conditions are specified in terms of forward invariance of a set, and are verified via two novel generalizations of barrier functions; in each case, the existence of a barrier function satisfying Lyapunov-like conditions implies forward invariance of the set, and the relationship between these two classes of barrier functions is characterized. In addition, each of these formulations yields a notion of control barrier function (CBF), providing inequality constraints in the control input that, when satisfied, again imply forward invariance of the set. Through these constructions, CBFs can naturally be unified with control Lyapunov functions (CLFs) in the context of a quadratic program (QP); this allows for the achievement of control objectives (represented by CLFs) subject to conditions on the admissible states of the system (represented by CBFs). The mediation of safety and performance through a QP is demonstrated on adaptive cruise control and lane keeping, two automotive control problems that present both safety and performance considerations coupled with actuator bounds.
